QHybrid Lab

A research demonstrator for quantum-classical hybrid machine learning: a network-flow triage model with its frozen decision policy, and a sandbox that compares a quantum-kernel classifier with classical models on identical inputs.

Research demonstrator, not a production detector. The model was validated on one corpus only, and its own frozen policy states it is not approved for deployment.

    What this is

    Two pieces of a larger research programme on where, if anywhere, quantum machine learning helps cybersecurity classification. The programme is classical-first: quantum research claims wait for a classical evidence gate. Until then the quantum part is shown only as this labelled sandbox.

    Triage model

    A LightGBM classifier on 29 behaviour features of NetFlow v2 records, with Platt calibration and three thresholds frozen on separate partitions. The service reproduces the research notebook's confusion matrix on all 478,055 validation flows, and refuses to start if any shipped file differs from its recorded SHA-256 hash.

    Hybrid lab protocol

    1. One stratified sample: up to 200 training rows and 200 test rows.
    2. Standard scaling → PCA to one component per qubit → rescaling to [0, 1], all fitted on training rows only.
    3. Logistic regression, linear SVM, RBF SVM and a quantum-kernel SVM are tuned on the same five folds with the same scorer, then scored once on the test rows.
    4. The quantum kernel is K(x, y) = |⟨ψ(bx)|ψ(by)⟩|² for a ZZ feature map, simulated exactly. Its bandwidth b is tuned like the RBF kernel's gamma.
    5. LightGBM on all original features of the same rows is shown as a classical control.

    Why bandwidth matters

    Without bandwidth tuning, quantum kernel values concentrate towards zero as qubits are added, and the classifier degrades towards guessing. Published analyses: Shaydulin & Wild, arXiv:2111.05451; Thanasilp, Wang, Cerezo & Holmes, Nature Communications (2024), doi:10.1038/s41467-024-49287-w.

    Limits

    • Noiseless statevector simulation; real hardware adds shot noise and gate errors, and would need the number of fidelity circuits shown under circuit resources.
    • Small samples; differences of a few points are within seed-to-seed variation.
    • No result here is evidence of quantum advantage, and none is claimed.
